01 · Who

I did not arrive at medicine with certainty. I followed what pulled me — the brain, the mystery of thought becoming biology — into neuroscience at UC San Diego, and then, almost by accident, into the rooms where science recedes and the person appears.

Two years in a lab studying mind-body interventions taught me what I cannot unsee: the moments I remember most vividly were never at the bench. They were with patients — people living with cancer, PTSD, ALS, MS — who taught me, sample by sample, what it means to be witnessed.

Now, as a Care Extender at UCLA, I am learning shift by shift that the difference between a doctor who survives and a doctor who transforms is not resilience in the way we usually mean it. It is the discipline to stay.

03 · The path so far

Not a résumé — a route. Stops along the way to staying present.

Tap any station to open it.

i
UC San Diego — Neuroscience
Followed the pull of the brain into a degree that gave me permission to have no fixed vision. Learned that the most interesting questions sit at the seam between mind and body.
+
ii
Lab — mind-body intervention research
Pipetted, ran assays, contributed to published work — and discovered that the saliva and cheek swabs were the easy part. What I remember is the patients on the other side of the sample.
+
iii
STARS Program — twenty months coordinating
Matched undergraduates with PhD mentors. Learned to listen across hierarchies, translate between faculty and students, and build structures that outlast any single coordinator.
+
iv
UCLA — Care Extender
Inside the machinery of medicine for four hours, once or twice a week. The silent weight of difficult news. A hand to hold post-surgery. The anger of the patient that is almost never about what it appears to be.
+
v
Meditation retreat, Florida — the yellow couch
The afternoon something loose finally locked into place. Not a question answered. A direction recognized.
